Let's talk

How much does political organizing literacy matter in our toolkit for advancing this work?
Venue literacy. Look at the tally above. Most of our IUI wins happen at a commission, but commissioners in all eight of these states are appointed, not elected. Do we track governors' races and legislative seats with the same rigor we track dockets?
Margin literacy. In North Carolina, two legislators supplied the exact supermajority that erased Duke's 2030 carbon target. Do we know who the swing votes are on the bills our programs depend on, and who organizes in their districts?
Ballot literacy. Missouri voters banned charging customers for unbuilt power plants in 1976. Lawmakers reversed it in 2025. In 2026, Missourians are using the referendum again on a different fight. When is a ballot measure the right tool, and when is it a trap?
Our lane. As a nonprofit, we can educate, testify, convene, and support nonpartisan voter engagement. We can't back candidates. Within that lane, what are we not doing yet that RECESS and RE-AMP participants told us mattered?
Facilitator note: this trivia is nonpartisan civic education. Facts about candidates and votes are included to show where energy decisions get made, not to support or oppose anyone.
